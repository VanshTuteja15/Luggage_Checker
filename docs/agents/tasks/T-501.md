## Goal (one paragraph, user-visible outcome)

In /admin → "Products to review", the admin works through the identity review queue quickly. Each listing is shown next to the product it may be, with the words that differ highlighted. The admin ticks several rows and decides them in one action ("Same product" / "Different product"), using the mouse or the keyboard. Since A5.3 the queue holds only real near matches: on the dev DB, 42 of the earlier 311, mostly a store's colour pages of one product (The North Face, Cole Haan, TUMI "Textured Finish"), so batches of a whole group are the normal case.

## Contract (types/endpoints to use; do not change them)

- `GET /api/admin/review?status=pending&limit=500` (admin only) → `{ items: ReviewItem[], advice: Record<reviewId, ReviewAdvice | null>, words: Record<reviewId, { onlyListing: string[]; onlyCandidate: string[] }> }`
  - `ReviewItem`: `id`, `listingId`, `listingTitle`, `listingUrl`, `sourceId`, `candidateProductId | null`, `candidateName | null`, `score | null`, `reasons: string[]` (`reasons[0]` is technical, e.g. `similar line: "…" vs "…"`; `reasons[1]`, when present, is the rule's plain sentence, e.g. "listed apart by the store; only the colour part differs"), `status`, `createdAt`
  - `ReviewAdvice` (only when the owner has turned on `ai.enabled` + `ai.match_judge`): `{ verdict: "same" | "variant" | "different", reason, model, at }`. It is advice; it is never applied.
  - `words`: product-name words only one side has (lower case), for highlighting.
- `POST /api/admin/review/batch` `{ ids: string[] (1–100), decision: "approved" | "rejected" }` → `{ results: { id, ok, error? }[] }`, one per id in order; 400 with `{ error }` for a bad body. Approve = "Same product" (the listing joins the candidate product, as a single click does); reject = "Different product" (kept apart).
- `POST /api/admin/review/<id>` `{ decision }` (single item, unchanged) may still be used for one row.
- Store names: `data.stores` (`sourceId` → `name`) as the current `Reviews` does.

## Requirements (numbered, testable)

1. When the tab opens, load once from the GET above (not from the overview's first 200). Show a plain loading line until it answers; on an error, the error text and a "Try again" button.
2. Group the rows by `candidateProductId`, largest group first. Group header: the candidate name linking to `/products/{candidateProductId}` (new tab), the number of rows, and a "Select group" checkbox. Rows without a candidate (rare now) go in a last group, "No product to compare with", where only "Different product" is offered.
3. Each row: a checkbox; the listing title linking to `listingUrl` (new tab, `rel="noopener noreferrer"`) and the store name; beneath it "may be: {candidateName}". Highlight the words from `words[id]`: `onlyListing` inside the listing title, `onlyCandidate` inside the candidate name. Use `<mark>` styled so it reads in light and dark mode, and match case-insensitively on whole words. Then `reasons[1]` in muted text when present. When `advice[id]` is non-null, add a muted line "AI advice (not applied): {verdict}, {reason}".
4. Selection: per row, per group, and "Select all shown", with the number selected always visible. Selection survives filtering, and decided rows leave the selection.
5. Actions on the selection: "Same product" and "Different product". Send at most 100 ids per request; split larger selections into sequential requests. Remove the rows whose result is `ok`. Keep failed rows with their `error` text shown in the row. Finish with one toast: "Joined 12." / "Kept 3 apart." / "2 couldn't be saved; see the rows."
6. "Same product" on more than 10 rows first asks inside the page (no `window.confirm`): "Join {n} listings to their products? Their prices move to that product; Split undoes it." with Join / Cancel.
7. Keyboard: `j` / `k` move the focused row, `x` toggles it, `s` = Same product and `d` = Different product for the selection (or the focused row when nothing is selected), `?` shows a short help line. Shortcuts never fire while typing in an input, select or textarea.
8. Filters: by store (select of the stores present) and a text filter over both names. Group and row counts follow the filters.
9. Empty state: keep today's sentence ("Nothing to review. When a store lists a bag we can't match for sure, it shows up here.").
10. Works at 375 px (rows stack, no horizontal scroll) and in dark mode. Every control is reachable by keyboard with a visible focus ring, and checkboxes and buttons have accessible names. Text stays selectable.
